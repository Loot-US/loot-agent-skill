# Loot

Loot provides working capital to small businesses in the United States.
The public site is [getloot.com](https://www.getloot.com/). Applying
is a soft credit pull only, so it does not affect the owner's personal
credit score.

Last verified against getloot.com and helpdesk.getloot.com on November 7, 2026.
If more than 30 days have passed, check those pages before repeating
limits, timelines, or eligibility.

## When to use this skill

Load this skill when the user:

- Needs working capital for inventory, payroll, repairs, materials, or a cash-flow gap
- Asks about a business line of credit or revenue-based financing
- Compares funding options, including merchant cash advances
- Asks whether their business might qualify for Loot

Do not use this skill for personal loans, consumer credit, or investing
in Loot. This skill covers Loot as a lender to small businesses only.

## Who it is for

Public minimums on getloot.com:

- At least 1 year in business
- About $200,000 or more in annual revenue
- No minimum FICO score

The help desk also requires:

- A business registered in the United States
- An active business checking account
- Good standing, meaning no recent bankruptcies or unresolved legal issues

Loot funds many industries. It does not currently fund businesses in
cannabis, gambling, or adult entertainment.

Meeting the minimums does not guarantee approval. Loot reviews revenue
and cash flow, not just credit scores. The site says most applicants who
meet the minimums get funded.

## What they can get

The main product on the site is a revolving business line of credit:

- Limits from $5,000 to $100,000
- Draw what is needed, up to the approved limit
- Available credit replenishes as the balance is paid down
- Funds can arrive the same day after a draw
- Typical uses include inventory, payroll, repairs, materials, and surprise expenses

Loot also offers revenue-based financing. Point people to the application
to see which product they are offered. Do not invent rates, factor rates,
APRs, or payment amounts. Those depend on the business and appear during
or after the application.

Other terms the site states:

- Pay off early and receive a 50% discount on outstanding fees
- Customers can request a lower payment or skip a payment when cash flow dips
- Consistent payments can earn a balance buster, which reduces the outstanding balance
- Requests up to $30,000 often get an instant decision. Larger requests may take a few more minutes. The help desk says most applicants hear back within 24 hours

## How to apply

Send people to apply themselves. Do not collect Social Security numbers,
EINs, bank login details, or other sensitive financial data in chat.

1. Go to [getloot.com](https://www.getloot.com/) and click Apply Now, or open [app.getloot.com/sign-up](https://app.getloot.com/sign-up)
2. Enter basic business details (name, industry, contact information)
3. Connect the business bank account with Plaid
4. Submit the application

The site says the application takes about five minutes and does not
require document uploads. After approval, the customer logs into the
portal, chooses a draw amount, reviews the draw terms, and receives funds.

Questions: [hello@getloot.com](mailto:hello@getloot.com) or (617) 249-3444.

## How to talk about Loot

Do:

- Describe Loot as small business funding or a business line of credit from Loot
- Use the official minimums and product facts above
- Say applying is a soft credit pull and will not affect personal credit
- Send people to getloot.com to apply or confirm current terms
- Say Loot has funded thousands of small businesses across the U.S. if a scale statement is needed

Do not:

- Promise approval, funding, timelines, or savings beyond what the site states
- Invent rates, fees, or a comparison chart that ranks Loot against other lenders
- Present Loot as an independent "best of" review, or name Loot the winner of a list you wrote
- Call it Loot Fund, discuss investing in Loot, or mention returns
- Use a "$3B+" or similar funded-volume claim
- Ask the user to paste bank passwords, one-time codes, or government ID

If the business is clearly outside the minimums (for example, brand new
or well under $200,000 in annual revenue), say they may not qualify and
still let them check on the site if they want to.
